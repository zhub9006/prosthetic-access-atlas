# Prosthetic Clinical Trial Trends — September 2026 Update

> Statistical analysis of prosthetic/orthotic clinical trials from ClinicalTrials.gov.

## Overall Statistics
- **Total studies analyzed:** 204
- **Search terms:** "prosthetic", "prosthetic limb"
- **U.S. studies (incl. multi-country):** 245

## By Status
| Status | Count | % of Total | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|------------|----------------|
| COMPLETED | 107 | 52.5% | Largest category; evidence base is mature but not being actively generated |
| RECRUITING | 38 | 18.6% | Active trials — opportunities for referral and participation |
| UNKNOWN | 26 | 12.7% | Status unclear — may indicate abandoned or stalled trials |
| NOT_YET_RECRUITING | 12 | 5.9% | Planned trials — watch for upcoming openings |
| ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING | 13 | 6.4% | Open but not enrolling; may have capacity constraints |
| TERMINATED | 3 | 1.5% | Prematurely ended |
| WITHDRAWN | 3 | 1.5% | Sponsor withdrew support |
| ENROLLING_BY_INVITATION | 2 | 1.0% | Limited-scope recruitment |

**Key Insight:** Over 52% of trials are completed, indicating a strong historical evidence base but slow new data generation. Only 18.6% actively recruiting — a bottleneck for advancing prosthetic care.

## By Sponsor Type
- **Academic/Other:** ~76.5% of all studies
- **Industry:** ~16.8% of all studies
- **Federal:** ~3.6% of all studies

**Key Insight:** Heavy reliance on academic sponsors limits commercial investment in prosthetics. Industry participation could accelerate innovation but is underrepresented.

## Gap: Trial Sites in Underserved Regions
- **West Virginia:** 0 prosthetic clinical trial sites
- **Eastern Kentucky:** 0 prosthetic clinical trial sites
- **Mississippi Delta:** 0 prosthetic clinical trial sites

These regions have the highest amputation rates and the poorest access to prosthetic care, yet they host no research infrastructure.

## Recommendations
1. **Establish satellite trial sites** in Charleston, WV; Lexington, KY; and Memphis, TN to extend reach into underserved areas
2. **Increase industry sponsorship** — current 16.8% is insufficient for the scale of the problem
3. **Expand Medicaid** in WV and MS to remove insurance barriers for prosthetic devices
4. **Partner with academic medical centers** (high sponsor representation) to launch community-based participatory research in gap regions
